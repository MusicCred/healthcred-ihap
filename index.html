What HealthCred Enrollment Unlocks

Real coverage. Real benefits. IHAP makes sure they're used.

When HealthCred enrolls someone in an ACA or Medicaid plan, they gain access to a full suite of healthcare benefits — many for the first time in their life. IHAP's role is making sure each person understands every benefit they have, knows how to use it, and actually does.

Mental Health Coverage

Therapy, counseling, psychiatric care, and crisis support — all covered. IHAP connects individuals to mental health providers before and after release, because the mind has to come first.

IHAP ensures: Provider connections + mental health literacy

Prescription Drug Coverage

Ongoing medications for chronic conditions, mental health, and recovery — covered from day one. IHAP helps bridge the gap so no one goes without medication during the coverage activation window.

IHAP ensures: Prescription assistance + pharmacy connections

Substance Use Treatment

Inpatient and outpatient substance use programs, MAT, and peer support — all ACA-covered. IHAP connects individuals to vetted recovery programs in their area the moment they leave.

IHAP ensures: Treatment referrals + MAT provider connections

Primary & Preventive Care

Annual checkups, screenings, immunizations, and chronic disease management — all covered. For many people we serve, this is the first time they've had a primary care doctor. IHAP helps them find one and use the relationship.

IHAP ensures: PCP connections + preventive care education

Emergency & Specialist Care

ER visits, specialist referrals, diagnostic imaging, and lab work — all covered. IHAP educates individuals on when and how to use emergency vs. primary care, reducing unnecessary ER visits and ensuring the right level of care.

IHAP ensures: Care navigation education + specialist referrals

Dental, Vision & Wellness

Dental care, vision correction, and wellness programs are often included in ACA plans and Medicaid. IHAP helps individuals understand what their specific plan covers and connects them to in-network providers near their release destination.

IHAP ensures: Benefits education + in-network provider matching

Our Story

We are the traction. Health is where everything starts.

IHAP exists because the resources were already there — coverage, community programs, mental health services — but people weren't reaching them. Not because they didn't want to, but because nobody was making the connection. IHAP is that connection. We help incarcerated individuals understand their healthcare, actually use it, and afford it. We connect them to mental health support so they can start getting their mind right while they're still inside. And we hand them off at exit to real people — community organizations, nonprofits, and programs that can provide the hands-on help that makes a difference in those first critical days. We're not in the business of recreating what's real. We're in the business of making sure people reach it.

IHAP works in partnership with HealthCred Care LLC, which operates the healthcare infrastructure inside correctional facilities across Florida, Georgia, and Alabama. HealthCred's platform identifies every individual at intake — the moment they arrive — flagging those with existing coverage and those without. For people without coverage, HealthCred connects them to IHAP immediately. That's where our work begins — not at release, but at the start of their stay.

When someone is released from a correctional facility, they qualify for a Special Enrollment Period (SEP) — a window under ACA rules that allows them to enroll in a marketplace health plan outside of the standard open enrollment calendar. IHAP uses that window, but the work starts long before the release date. Our counselors go in at intake, sit with people during their stay, gather every document needed, and build the complete application — so the moment they walk out, submission is immediate. Coverage typically activates within 30 days. And IHAP is there for every one of those days, bridging the gap so no one goes without while they wait.

Research published in The Lancet found that in the first two weeks after release, a person's risk of death is 12.7 times higher than the general population — with drug overdose as the leading cause. That's not a failure of character. It's a failure of infrastructure. People walk out with untreated conditions, no medication, and nowhere to turn. IHAP closes that gap — enrollment application ready to submit at release, a care plan built before the door opens, and bridge support in place for every day while coverage activates.

We also bridge the window while coverage activates — prescription assistance, community clinic connections, and immediate care resources — because those first few weeks are when people are most vulnerable and most likely to fall through the cracks. Research shows that insurance within the first 30 days of release is associated with 60% lower odds of returning to incarceration.

Even after the ACA, more than 30% of adults with recent justice involvement remain uninsured — double the general population rate. In Medicaid expansion states, 80–90% of incarcerated people are already eligible. The barrier isn't eligibility. It's enrollment. That's exactly what IHAP does, at no cost to the individuals we serve.

"When someone walks out of a jail without healthcare, the odds are already stacked against them. Giving someone coverage and a care plan before they leave isn't charity — it's the most effective intervention we have to keep them out, keep their families together, and keep communities safe."

Chad R. LaBoy Founder & President, IHAP

What Drives People Back

Three things that fuel the revolving door — and how IHAP breaks it

01
🏥

No Coverage Means No Treatment

Federal law suspends Medicaid the moment someone is incarcerated. When they're released, they walk out with no insurance and no path to a doctor. More than 30% of adults with recent justice involvement remain uninsured — double the general population rate. A condition that $50 a month would manage becomes a crisis that sends someone back to jail and costs the system tens of thousands of dollars. IHAP solves this at the source: using the ACA's Special Enrollment Period (SEP) that triggers at release, we prepare every application during the person's stay — so it's submitted the moment they walk out. Coverage takes up to 30 days to activate, and IHAP bridges every one of those days. Nobody has to figure this out alone, after the fact, from a sidewalk.

02
🔄

No Care Plan Means No Safety Net

63% of people in jail have a substance use disorder. 44% have a diagnosable mental health condition. People with both have a 68% rate of returning to jail within 4 years — the highest of any group. These conditions don't go away at release. Without a care plan, a referral, or anyone to call, the next crisis lands them right back inside. IHAP builds that safety net before release day — a doctor, a care coordinator, a plan — not after the first emergency.

03
🏠

No Stability Means No Future

You can't keep a doctor's appointment from a shelter. You can't manage a prescription without an address. In the first year after release, formerly incarcerated people average 3.6 emergency room visits — using the ER as their primary care because they have no stable base to build from. Housing instability unravels every other form of progress. IHAP provides housing coordination and stabilization support for the people most at risk, because a stable home is where everything else — health, family, community — becomes possible again.

How We Help

We meet people where they are — inside, and on the way out.

IHAP goes into jails and does something simple but rare: we sit with people, we listen, and we help them get covered before they leave. Your gift makes that possible.

How We Help 01

SEP Enrollment — Prepared Inside, Submitted at Release

When someone is released from a correctional facility, federal rules create a Special Enrollment Period (SEP) — a legal window to enroll in ACA marketplace coverage outside of open enrollment. IHAP goes inside at intake and works with people throughout their stay — gathering documents, completing applications, and building the full enrollment file. The moment they're released, the application goes in immediately. Coverage activates within 30 days. For most people we serve, it's the first healthcare card of their adult life — and they didn't have to figure it out alone, from nothing, after the door already closed behind them.

How We Help 02

Bridging the Gap to Care

The two weeks after release are the most dangerous period in a person's life — research shows the risk of death is 12.7 times higher than the general population during this window. Coverage doesn't always activate immediately, so IHAP bridges that gap: prescription assistance, community clinic connections, urgent care referrals, and medication cost support. Nobody goes without care while they're waiting for their card to activate.

How We Help 03

Building a Care Plan for Life Outside

A healthcare card means nothing without someone to use it with. Our care coordinators connect people to a primary care doctor, behavioral health services, substance use treatment, and community providers — building a complete care plan before release day, not after the first crisis lands someone back in the ER.

How We Help 04

Helping People Find Stable Ground

Without a stable place to live, everything else falls apart. IHAP provides housing coordination and stabilization support for people at the highest risk of homelessness after release — because we know that a stable home is where real recovery begins. Health and housing go together, and we treat them that way.

Why It Matters Beyond the Individual

When one person gets healthcare and stays out, the whole community wins.

The research is unambiguous. Studies show that Medicaid access is linked to a 30% reduction in violent crime recidivism and a 24% reduction in public-order crime. People with insurance within 30 days of release have 60% lower odds of returning. Continuity of care — a doctor, a prescription, a person to call — is the single most evidence-based intervention we have for keeping people out.

And this isn't just about one person. Every person who gets care and stays out is a parent home for their kids, a partner who doesn't leave, a neighbor who becomes part of the community instead of cycling back through the system. The math is undeniable — and so is the human case.

🏘️

Measurably Safer Communities

Peer-reviewed research links Medicaid enrollment at release to a 30% reduction in violent crime recidivism and 24% fewer public-order reoffenses. This isn't theory — it's documented in published studies. IHAP creates the coverage and care continuity that drives those numbers.

👨‍👩‍👧

Families Kept Together

10 million children in the U.S. have a parent who has been incarcerated. Every person who stays out is a parent present, a partner supported, a sibling no longer absent. The ripple of one person getting the care they need reaches every person connected to them.

💰

Enormous Savings for Taxpayers

A single year in county jail costs $45,000–$53,000 per person. People with justice involvement make up just 4.2% of the U.S. population but account for 8.5% of all ER spending — an estimated $5.2 billion in excess costs annually. In Lee County, IHAP documented $5.9M in savings in its first year. Prevention is always less expensive than crisis.
